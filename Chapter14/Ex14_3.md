### Exercise 14.3

If we have a basis matrix for contact coordinates C, and a basis matrix for an object's local coordinates M, write an expression for transforming the object's inertia tensor I into contact coordinates.

### Solution

$$
I' = C^{-1} \cdot M \cdot I \cdot M^{-1} \cdot C
$$

Why?



A **coordinate basis matrix** can indeed transform a vector from a local or object space into the global space (or another coordinate space).

**Applying the Transformation to the Inertia Tensor**:
 The inertia tensor is essentially a **linear operator** that takes one vector and returns another. To see how it transforms, we need to consider how the vector components transform and how the inertia tensor interacts with those transformed components.

- **Left multiplication** by $P^{-1}$: This transforms the vector in the **input** (the original frame) into the new coordinate system.
- **Right multiplication** by $P$: This transforms the vector in the **output** (the resulting vector from the inertia tensor) into the new coordinate system.

The inertia tensor $I$ is not just a scalar—it relates two vectors (it’s a 2D array of numbers), so you need to apply the transformation matrix to both sides of the tensor to get the correct transformed result.

**The Rule $I' = P^{-1} \cdot I \cdot P$**:

- The matrix $P^{-1}$ is applied to the **input** vector (i.e., the vector you’re transforming) to convert it to the new frame.
- The matrix $P$ is applied to the **output** of the tensor, ensuring that the transformed inertia tensor $I'$ correctly reflects the new coordinate system.
